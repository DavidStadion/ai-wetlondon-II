Here is a thing that happens to me roughly once a month. We go somewhere, usually for no particular reason, and about ten minutes in I look around at the low ceiling and the candles and some ancient part of my brain quietly files it under "this would be a great date".

Which is, I want to stress, completely useless information. I am not going on a date. I have been married a good while. My evenings are mostly a negotiation about whose turn it is to do the thing neither of us wants to do.

But the filing cabinet is already there, and it is still working, and it seems a waste for nobody to benefit from it. So here are eight places that set it off, all of them indoors, on the grounds that being rained on is nobody's idea of a promising start.

## 1. Gordon's Wine Bar

A candlelit cellar off the Embankment that has been pouring wine since the 1890s and has not troubled a lightbulb since. It is so dark down there that you cannot really see the person opposite you, which is either deeply romantic or a mercy, depending.

The catch is everyone else has also worked this out. You will hover. You will make eye contact with a table that looks like it might be leaving and it will not be leaving. Go early, accept the hovering as part of the experience, and it is the best room in London for this.

[Gordon's Wine Bar](/venue/gordons-wine-bar)

## 2. Ronnie Scott's Jazz Club

Soho, small tables, very good musicians, and a firm expectation that you will shut up while they play.

That last part is the real feature. Two hours where talking is actively discouraged is a gift on any date past the second one, when the prepared material has run out and you are both fishing. You sit in the dark, you listen to something excellent, and you leave with something to talk about on the way home.

[Ronnie Scott's Jazz Club](/venue/ronnie-scotts-jazz-club)

## 3. Electric Cinema Notting Hill

Leather armchairs, footstools, blankets, and someone bringing you a drink in your seat.

The only genuine risk here is that the chairs are so comfortable you fall asleep on somebody you are actively trying to impress. Sit up. Order a coffee. It is a beautiful room and it is worth staying conscious for.

[Electric Cinema Notting Hill](/venue/electric-cinema-notting-hill)

## 4. The Viktor Wynd Museum

A basement in Hackney containing, among other things, taxidermy of questionable legality, occult ephemera, and a cocktail bar. It costs ten pounds.

This is the diagnostic one. If your date walks down those stairs, looks at a shelf of jarred oddities and lights up, you have found something out about them very early and very cheaply. If they do not, you have also found something out. Either way, ten pounds well spent.

[The Viktor Wynd Museum](/venue/the-viktor-wynd-museum)

## 5. Lucky Voice Karaoke

Private booths, no stage, no audience, and a button on the wall that summons more drinks without you having to go and ask for them.

Karaoke on a date is high risk and high information. The private booth is what makes it survivable: whatever happens in there is contained, witnessed by nobody, and legally deniable. The button is genuinely one of the great pieces of design in London hospitality.

[Lucky Voice Karaoke](/venue/lucky-voice-karaoke)

## 6. Marshall Street Leisure Centre

A restored 1930s swimming pool in the middle of Soho, all marble and barrel-vaulted ceiling, for about six quid.

Suggesting a swim as a date is objectively an unhinged thing to do and I am aware of that. But it is a genuinely beautiful listed building, it is the cheapest thing on this list by a distance, and anyone who says yes to it is somebody worth knowing.

[Marshall Street Leisure Centre](/venue/marshall-street-leisure-centre)

## 7. Mercato Mayfair

A food hall inside a converted church, which is a sentence London produces without blinking.

The quiet genius of a food hall is that you both eat entirely different things, which removes the single most tedious negotiation available to two people in a restaurant. No compromise dish. No pretending you also wanted the sharing plates. Free to walk into, and the building is worth it on its own.

[Mercato Mayfair](/venue/mercato-mayfair)

## 8. Sketch Afternoon Tea

Seventy-five pounds. A pink room. Toilets shaped like eggs.

I am not going to construct an argument that this is good value, because it is not. It is enormously expensive tea in a room designed principally to be photographed. But it is also unlike anywhere else, everybody who goes comes back talking about it, and there is a certain kind of date where the correct move is simply to be ridiculous on purpose.

[Sketch Afternoon Tea](/venue/sketch-afternoon-tea)

## What all this not dating has taught me

Looking back at that list, not one of them is romantic in the way the word is usually sold. No rose petals. Nothing with a violin.

What they have in common is that they all hand you something to talk about, so neither of you has to sit there generating conversation out of nothing. That is the actual job. A good date venue does some of the work for you.

Which, and I say this with the full authority of a man whose dating career ended some time ago, is also the entire trick to being married. Keep going places that give you something to say to each other.
