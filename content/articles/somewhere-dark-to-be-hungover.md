The hungover brief is unusually specific, and most listings sites are no help with it whatsoever, because they keep recommending things with stimulation in them.

What is actually required is a short list. It must be dark. You must be sitting down. It must be warm, because being cold while hungover is a distinct and worse condition. It must be quiet, or at least the noise must be somebody else's problem. And crucially, once you are in, nobody must ask you to make another decision, because you have used up your capacity for those.

Rain helps, oddly. It removes the guilt about the day, which is half the battle.

Here are seven, in order of how much dark they provide.

## Maximum dark, minimum money: Prince Charles Cinema

Twelve pounds, two minutes from Leicester Square, cult classics and sing-alongs.

The Prince Charles is the correct answer more often than anywhere else in central London. It is cheap enough that falling asleep is not a financial event, the programming is films you have already seen, and no part of the experience requires you to be a functioning adult. Check what is on, but honestly it barely matters.

[Prince Charles Cinema](/venue/prince-charles-cinema)

## Dark, and you are lying down: Electric Cinema Notting Hill

Twenty-five pounds, sofas and footstools, five minutes from Notting Hill Gate.

This is the expensive version of the same idea and worth it exactly once per hangover season. Armchairs, blankets, a bar, and the general atmosphere of a very comfortable living room that somebody else is heating. You will not remember the film.

[Electric Cinema Notting Hill](/venue/electric-cinema-notting-hill)

## Dark, and there is a department store attached: The Cinema at Selfridges

Twenty pounds, a boutique screen inside the shop.

The specific advantage here is logistics. You are already indoors, in a warm building with food in it, and the walk from the door to the seat involves no weather and no further planning. If the group cannot agree on anything, this building absorbs all of you.

[The Cinema at Selfridges](/venue/the-cinema-at-selfridges)

## Dark, and slightly more serious: Curzon Soho

Fourteen pounds, new releases and arthouse, in Soho.

Includes for the sake of honesty a warning: the Curzon programmes things that require attention. If your hangover is at the stage where subtitles are an act of violence, go one entry up. If you are on the mend and want to feel like a person again, this is the one that does it.

[Curzon Soho](/venue/curzon-soho)

## Dark, and nowhere near the centre: Phoenix Cinema

Fourteen pounds, one of London's oldest cinemas, cosy and properly local.

North London, which is the point. Part of the hungover condition is not wanting to see the crowds in zone one, and the Phoenix is a small warm room a long way from all of that. It has been showing films since 1912, so it has seen worse than you.

[Phoenix Cinema](/venue/phoenix-cinema)

## Not dark, but weirdly perfect: Horniman Museum

Free, in Forest Hill, with an overstuffed walrus in it.

The walrus is famous for being wrong. The Victorians who stuffed it had never seen a live one and did not know walruses are meant to be wrinkly, so they filled it until it was smooth and enormous. It is the single most calming thing in south London to stand in front of when you feel terrible, and it costs nothing.

[Horniman Museum](/venue/horniman-museum)

## The nuclear option: ESPA Life at Corinthia

A hundred and fifty pounds, a luxury spa with a pool, three minutes from Embankment.

Included because somebody reading this has had the sort of night where a hundred and fifty pounds seems reasonable at eleven the next morning. It is not reasonable. It will, however, work.

[ESPA Life at Corinthia](/venue/espa-life-at-corinthia)
