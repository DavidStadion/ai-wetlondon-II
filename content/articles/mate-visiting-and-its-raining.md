There is a very particular pressure to this one.

Someone has spent actual money and actual annual leave to come and look at the city you happen to live in. They have arrived with a mental image assembled entirely from films, in which London is all fog and red buses and someone running dramatically across a bridge. And you have woken up to a sky the colour of dishwater and a forecast that says this is it now, all week.

The wrong instinct, which I have had many times, is to apologise for the weather and then overcorrect by marching them down Oxford Street to prove the city still works. Oxford Street in the rain has ended friendships. Do not do this.

The right move is to pick things that are either better in the rain or completely indifferent to it. Here are five, in the order I would actually use them.

## 1. Sky Garden

Free, at the top of the Walkie Talkie, and entirely behind glass.

The thing about a view in London is that you normally have to stand out in the weather to earn it, which rather undercuts the moment. Here you are indoors with a drink, warm, looking down at the rain doing its business to everybody else. On a grey day the city looks moody and enormous and exactly like the version your friend was promised.

Book ahead. It is free, which means it is booked solid, which is the most London sentence in this article.

[Sky Garden](/venue/sky-garden)

## 2. Churchill War Rooms

Underground, which is the most comprehensively rain-proof format available to a building.

It is the actual bunker, left more or less as it was when they walked out of it in 1945, down to the pins in the maps. Your friend will go in doing the polite tourist face and come out quiet, which is the highest compliment a museum ever gets. Twenty-six pounds and worth every one of them.

[Churchill War Rooms](/venue/churchill-war-rooms)

## 3. Leadenhall Market

A Victorian covered market in the middle of the City that looks like a film set, largely because it has been one.

Painted ironwork, cobbles, glass roof, and the specific pleasure of hearing rain hammering on something a long way above your head. Half an hour and a drink in here will produce more photographs than anything you have paid to get into, and it costs nothing at all.

[Leadenhall Market](/venue/leadenhall-market)

## 4. The Wallace Collection

A mansion off Oxford Street stuffed with Old Masters, French furniture and a genuinely excessive quantity of armour. It is free, and almost nobody queues for it.

This is the one that makes you look good. You will have walked your friend past the crowds outside the big names, in the rain, and delivered them into a warm townhouse full of world-class paintings with room to breathe. They will assume you have impeccable taste. It cost you nothing. Say nothing.

[The Wallace Collection](/venue/the-wallace-collection)

## 5. Borough Market

Food, under Victorian railway arches, next to London Bridge.

Honest caveat, because that is the entire point of this website: this is the wettest thing on the list. The arches cover a lot of it but not all of it, and you will catch a bit of weather moving between stalls. Save it for when the rain drops to a drizzle. Then eat your way along it, because almost every mood a jetlagged visitor can be in is improved by being fed.

[Borough Market](/venue/borough-market)

## The bit that actually matters

The mistake is treating the rain as a thing to be survived, apologised for and worked around.

London is a city that was built by people who knew exactly what the weather was going to do. That is why it has arcades and covered markets and deep basements and vast free museums you can lose an afternoon in. The rain is not an interruption to the city. It is one of the reasons the city looks the way it does.

Take somebody to a place that only really works because it is raining, and they will not go home thinking they got unlucky with the forecast. They will go home thinking you live somewhere brilliant.
