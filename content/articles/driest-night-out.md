Every poster for a West End show sells you the same things: the awards, the songs you already know, a photograph of somebody mid-leap.

None of them sell you the actual proposition, which on a January Tuesday is this. You go inside a building. You give somebody your coat. You sit in a warm room for three hours while something expensive happens in front of you. Then you come out and it is over, and you have not once considered the sky.

Restaurants make you leave between courses and the good ones are a walk from the tube. Pubs are fine until the smokers open the door. Cinemas are close, but a film is a film and you can have one at home. Theatre is the only evening in London where the roof is part of the product and nobody thinks to mention it.

Here is the case, by price.

## If you have never done it: Matilda

Twenty-four pounds, the Cambridge Theatre by Seven Dials, three minutes from Covent Garden station.

Tim Minchin wrote the songs, which is the detail that converts sceptics: they are much funnier and much nastier than a family musical has any right to be. The set is built out of alphabet blocks and the whole thing is over in two and a half hours. If you have decided musicals are not for you, this is the one that argues back.

[Matilda The Musical](/venue/matilda-the-musical)

## If you want the big one: Les Misérables

Thirty pounds and up, the Sondheim on Shaftesbury Avenue, five minutes from Piccadilly Circus.

Running since 1985, which means it has outlasted the entire pop career of most people reading this. Nearly three hours, one interval, and a barricade that still does the thing. Go in knowing that everyone dies and you will still be unprepared for how it lands.

[Les Misérables](/venue/les-miserables)

## If you want to be genuinely surprised: Hadestown

Thirty-three pounds, the Lyric, four minutes from Piccadilly Circus.

Orpheus and Eurydice, retold as New Orleans jazz in a bar at the end of the world, which sounds insufferable and is not. Eight Tony awards and the best band in the West End, playing on stage all night. The one to book if you think you have seen what a musical can do.

[Hadestown](/venue/hadestown)

## If you want to laugh at something rude: The Book of Mormon

Twenty-five pounds, the Prince of Wales, three minutes from Piccadilly Circus.

From the people who made South Park, and much warmer than that suggests. Genuinely, properly filthy in places. Know your companion before booking, because there is no interval long enough to recover from a misjudgement here.

[The Book of Mormon](/venue/the-book-of-mormon)

## If you want the stagecraft: Stranger Things: The First Shadow

Thirty pounds, the Phoenix on Charing Cross Road, four minutes from Tottenham Court Road.

You do not need to have watched the series. What you are going for is the technical work, because things happen in that theatre that should not be possible without a camera and an edit, and the audience gasps out loud roughly every twenty minutes. Over twelves only.

[Stranger Things: The First Shadow](/venue/stranger-things-the-first-shadow)

## If you want to spend nothing: Angel Comedy

Free. Genuinely free, upstairs and downstairs at a pub in Islington, seven nights a week.

There is a bucket at the end and you should put something in it, but the ticket price is nothing and the standard is startling, because this is where people work out material before they take it anywhere that charges. Turn up early, it fills.

[Angel Comedy](/venue/angel-comedy)

## If you want the building more than the show: Wilton's Music Hall

Fifteen pounds and up, in a lane off Cable Street in Whitechapel.

The oldest surviving grand music hall in the world, saved from demolition twice, and deliberately left looking every one of its years: peeling paint, bare brick, nothing restored to a shine. Whatever is on, the room is the reason to go.

[Wilton's Music Hall](/venue/wiltons-music-hall)

## The bit the posters leave out

Book a matinee if the forecast is properly grim. You go in while it is still light and awful, you come out and it is dark and awful, and you have removed the worst four hours of the day without noticing them.

Also: the cloakroom. Every West End theatre has one, most charge a couple of pounds, and almost nobody uses it. Hand over the wet coat. Sitting through three hours with a damp anorak on your lap is a self-inflicted injury and the fix costs less than the interval ice cream.
